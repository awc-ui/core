## Intent

**Problem:** Three stories measured the same thing on the iOS simulator. Story 1-2 (jobs 071 to 074): every states miss of md-search lies in a block of the main thread at the press, `UITextField` `becomeFirstResponder` with `_reloadInputViews`, 36 to 57 ms, also in a warm process. Story 9-20 (job 008): 32 of 40 failing md-text-field checks lie in a block of 30 to 59 ms that starts 0 to 15 ms after the input; the product already starts its transition at the press; 4 of 10 runs on iPhone 26.3 and 2 of 5 on iOS 18.6 are clean. Story 6-2 (KNOWN-CAUSES row 35, job 037): the first HID key of a process creates UIKit's keyboard object once, 561 ms; its priming attempt (jobs 038 to 041, `KEYPRIME ms=13`) removed one outlier and left 2 to 4 first-phase misses per iPad run. Story 9-6 has first-attempt misses of 30 to 34 ms at Tab and one paste miss with an 89 ms block. The gap rule of the states suites compares within about [t-40, t+34] ms, so a block of 30 to 59 ms gives a miss in some runs and none in others. No frame can be drawn while the main thread is inside UIKit, and the products cannot avoid the call at that moment. Nobody has measured how much of each block is UIKit's own time, whether it grows with the load of the Mac, or how the suites could treat it without hiding a late product.

**Approach:** Step 1, measure, with no rule changed. In the shim of the private tree, bracket the time the main thread spends inside UIKit's `becomeFirstResponder` and `resignFirstResponder` and inside the first key's keyboard creation, and record the intervals (start, end, and the time spent in product callbacks inside them, if that can be separated). Run the md-text-field states suite with the gap trace ten times as usual and ten times starting only when at most one other job is in `claude-runner/running` (wait up to 20 minutes; do not stop other jobs, do not create load), and log the Mac load per second. Report: the length of the bracketed intervals in both sets, and for every failing check whether its missing frames lie inside a bracketed interval. Step 2, decide from those numbers and write the decision into the record. If the quiet runs have intervals below the window and are all clean, the cause is the load of the Mac: stop with `blocked` and the numbers; the coordinator decides about exclusive running. Otherwise write one shared helper for the suites that use the gap rule: a Web sample whose time falls inside a bracketed UIKit interval (extended by one frame for the commit) is compared with the first native frame drawn after the interval, against the Web value at that frame's own time, with the tolerance the suite already uses; every other sample is compared as before; each run prints how many samples were treated this way. Step 3, three mutation runs: the product's transition start delayed by 34 ms must fail; a 50 ms sleep in the product's focus handler, outside the bracket, must fail; with the bracket switched off the old misses must return. Step 4, the first key: either make the keyboard creation happen before the first phase (the `KEYPRIME` line must then show the creation time, not 13 ms) or bracket it like the responder change; show the first-phase misses of the time-picker suite on the iPad before and after. Step 5, regression on iPhone 26.3 over the iOS states suites that story 9-14 job 053 ran, before and after: no suite loses a passing check; list the checks that newly pass and the number of treated samples per suite.
