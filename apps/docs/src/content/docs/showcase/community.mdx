**Corvus is the app about people rather than pictures.** A three-column feed of
what your friends are saying, the friendships behind it, the groups they meet
in, the events they turn up to — and threaded arguments underneath all of it,
built entirely from AWC UI components.

## The five builds

All five are live, and the control bar switches between them without losing your
place. Every person, post, comment, group and timestamp comes from
`@awc-ui/showcase-kit`, so the only thing that differs between them is the view
layer — which is what makes two screenshots comparable evidence.

| Build | Open | How it renders | Where the language lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HTML** | [/html/](/showcase/community/html/) | Static pages, written at build time — no framework at all | **In the URL** — a page per locale |
| **React** | [/react/](/showcase/community/react/) | Single-page app — Vite, no meta-framework | Client state — switches in place |
| **Vue** | [/vue/](/showcase/community/vue/) | Single-page app — Vite, no meta-framework | Client state — switches in place |
| **Angular** | [/angular/](/showcase/community/angular/) | Single-page app — client-rendered only | Client state — switches in place |
| **Svelte** | [/svelte/](/showcase/community/svelte/) | Single-page app — Vite, no meta-framework | Client state — switches in place |

:::note[Seven states where a "like" would have been two]
The reaction is the smallest thing on the screen and the one that justifies the
whole data model. A like is a boolean. Six reactions plus "none" is seven
states, and the case that gets written wrong is not picking one — it is the
**switch**: a reader moving from Like to Love has not reacted twice, so two
counts move at once and the total must not budge.

That arithmetic lives in `reactionSummary()` in the kit, once, so five builds
cannot each get it slightly different. Every build's browser test presses the
same sequence — pick, switch, take back — and asserts the total returns to
exactly where it started.

The same shape recurs everywhere in this vertical. A friendship is not a
boolean either: it is five states with a gap between asking and being answered,
and the two ends of that gap need opposite verbs, or you can accept your own
request. Group membership adds **pending**, which is the entire reason privacy
is data rather than a lock icon — pressing Join on a private group must leave
you asking, not joined.
:::

## What it is really demonstrating

A social network was chosen because it asks for what a photo feed does not:
**depth instead of breadth**. Lyra's data is wide and shallow — many posts, one
level of comments. Corvus is the opposite.

- **Comments that nest, and a thread that knows when to stop.** Replies go two
  levels deep in reading order, and "Replying to X" appears at depth 2 only:
  at depth 1 the indent is unambiguous, one possible parent directly above, so
  a label on every reply would be noise on most of them.
- **A post inside a post.** A share renders the original in full, with its
  author — and deliberately without its actions, because pressing a reaction on
  the inner post would react to something the reader is not looking at. It is
  also not wrapped in a link, which would put an anchor inside an anchor.
- **Text-first, so the card cannot be built around a picture.** Twenty-seven of
  forty-eight posts carry no image at all; six are link previews and four are
  shares. A card designed around a photograph gets all three of those wrong.
- **`-webkit-line-clamp` and a "see more" that is real.** A long body clamps at
  a line boundary rather than a character count, and un-clamps in place.
- **A three-column shell that drops a column before it drops the layout.** The
  right rail — contacts, birthdays, upcoming events — goes at 1200px; the shell
  itself swaps at 900. Two breakpoints because they answer different questions.
- **Three languages including a right-to-left one, and the CONTENT is
  translated.** Post bodies, comments, bios and group descriptions are
  dictionary entries, not fixture strings, so an Arabic reader gets an Arabic
  network rather than Arabic chrome around English prose.

:::tip[What the plain-HTML build had to solve twice]
The static build has no router and no state, so anything that appears on a press
has to already be in the page — and a hidden `<div>` is still an element the
parity census counts. Comment threads, the rest of the feed and the open
composer therefore live in `<template>` elements, whose contents are an inert
fragment outside the document, and are cloned in on demand.

That single decision produced both of the bugs found after this vertical was
otherwise finished. A clone arrives with no `hidden` attribute, so a toggle that
read the attribute and inverted it **hid the thread the reader had just asked
for**. And every binder shared one `data-bound` flag, so the like binder claimed
the aggregate "View all N comments" button — which wears the same class for its
styling — and the thread binder skipped it as already bound. Both looked
identical from outside: press for comments, get no comments.

Each binder now claims its own key, and anything that opens a thread states the
state it wants rather than flipping whatever it finds. The lesson generalises:
an element that appears **later** needs its own assertion, because a listener
that was never attached looks exactly like a reader who has not pressed
anything.
:::

## Fictional people

Corvus, its twenty-six people, their posts, comments, groups and events are
invented, and the warning is load-bearing here for the same reason it is on
Lyra: this vertical invents PEOPLE, with opinions and arguments. That is why the
disclaimer sits in the app bar on every screen rather than at the bottom of one.
No real person, place, group or event is represented, and none of the artwork
depicts anyone.

The fixture holds 26 people, 10 groups, 12 events, 48 posts and 286 comments
over 65 generated images, all from one seed and internally consistent — a reply
always answers a comment on the same post, every group's member count reconciles
with its roster, and roughly seventy invariants are asserted at generation time
rather than hoped for. Every state in every union is populated, including the
awkward ones: a private group you are not in, so join-becomes-pending is
reachable; requests pending in both directions; and an event that is online.

Every timestamp is measured from a fixed reporting instant of **14 October
2026, 17:40 UTC**, not from the clock.
