A component gallery shows you parts. These are whole applications: real
navigation, real drill-downs, real data density — the conditions a component
library actually has to survive.

Each one is built once and shipped in **every framework it targets** — React,
Vue, Angular, Svelte and plain HTML with no framework at all, plus Astro and the
four server-rendered builds on the credit-risk console. Open one in React,
switch to Angular or Svelte from inside the app, and you are looking at the same
screens and the same data rendered by a different stack. Not a port each time:
the data, the translations, the derived series and the table layouts all come
from one shared package, so the only thing that differs between the builds is
the view layer.

The control bar in each app also flips **theme, density, text direction and
language** at runtime, so you can see how the components behave under conditions
most demos never show.

## Applications

### [Credit Risk Console](/showcase/credit-risk/)

A wholesale-banking credit risk workbench for the fictional **Aurelia Bank** —
portfolio exposure that drills down through sectors, counterparties, facilities
and covenants, with a watchlist and stress-testing scenarios.

Built to stress the parts of a library that dashboards usually avoid: dense
sortable tables, deep breadcrumb trails, charts fed from the same data, corporate
group hierarchies, and a full translation layer including a right-to-left
language.

Ten builds: each framework twice, once as a single-page app and once
server-rendered per request.

### [Wealth Management Console](/showcase/wealth/)

A private-bank advisory workbench for the fictional **Kestrel Private Bank** — a
book of households that opens out into portfolios, positions, rebalancing
proposals, an order ticket and a goal planner.

Built to stress what a dashboard never asks for: a four-step form that vetoes
its own step changes until each is valid, an order ticket that re-estimates as
you type, a transfer list whose universe prunes itself as the constraints above
it change, and an organisation chart that traces a selection back to its root.

Five builds, all single-page or static — no SSR. The plain-HTML one is the
comparison worth making: no framework, no hydration of its own, a page per
locale written at build time.

### [Money & Investing App](/showcase/banking/)

A consumer banking and investing app for the fictional **Vela** — five accounts,
a year of statement, an exchange desk, a small portfolio and the cards that
spend it.

The two consoles above are workstation software. This one is a phone app that
also has to work on a desktop screen, which is a different set of demands: two
screens swap their markup rather than their styling below 720px, every touch
target grows under `pointer: coarse`, and a statement grouped by day has to
re-derive each day's total whenever a filter changes what is under the heading.

Five builds, all single-page or static. The plain-HTML one answers the same
media queries with no framework to re-render it.

### [Photos & People](/showcase/social/)

A social photo app for the fictional **Lyra** — a feed of pictures from the
people you follow, an explore grid over everything else, a four-step composer,
notifications and the profiles behind all of it.

The other three are figures. This one is content: pictures with aspect ratios
that must not reflow, captions and comments that are themselves translated
rather than being fixture strings, timestamps in "3h ago" measured against a
fixed instant, and a carousel whose pager sits inside a card whose picture is a
link. Every image is SVG artwork generated from the seed and inlined — not one
photograph, no CDN, and every one of them carries a written description.

Five builds, all single-page or static. The plain-HTML one keeps its state
swaps in `<template>` elements, because a hidden element is still an element.

### [Friends & Groups](/showcase/community/)

A social network for the fictional **Corvus** — a three-column feed of what your
friends are saying, the friendships behind it, the groups they meet in, the
events they turn up to, and threaded arguments underneath all of it.

Lyra is wide and shallow: many pictures, one level of comments. This one is the
opposite. Comments nest two deep in reading order, a share renders the original
post inside the outer one, and almost nothing here is a boolean — a reaction is
seven states whose *switch* must leave the total alone, a friendship is five
with a gap between asking and being answered, and group membership adds
**pending**, which is why privacy is data rather than a lock icon.

Five builds, all single-page or static. The plain-HTML one clones threads, feed
pages and the composer out of `<template>` elements on demand, because a hidden
element is still an element the parity census counts.

### [Music & Studio](/showcase/music/)

A music app for the fictional **Cygnus** — a listening half with a library,
albums and artists, an editing half with an arrangement timeline and a mixer,
and a transport bar that spans both.

The other five hold their interaction state inside a screen. This one cannot: an
app that stopped playing when you opened an album would not be a music app, so
the transport lives above the router in all four SPA builds — and the
plain-HTML one, which has no router at all, writes it to `sessionStorage` and
reads it back on every page load. Time is the layout's axis rather than a row
index, and the arrangement is a CSS grid with one column per bar because the
deployed policy forbids the `style` attribute a pixel offset would need.

Five builds, all single-page or static. Nothing plays: there is no audio, and
the playhead advances only while the transport says it is playing.
